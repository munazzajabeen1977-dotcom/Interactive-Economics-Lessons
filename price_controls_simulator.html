A ceiling below equilibrium or a floor above it is binding — set it on the "wrong" side of equilibrium and it stops doing anything
How to read it: at the control price, quantity demanded and quantity supplied usually differ — the market can only trade the smaller of the two. A binding ceiling (like rent control) sets quantity supplied below quantity demanded, causing a shortage. A binding floor (like minimum wage) sets quantity demanded below quantity supplied, causing a surplus. Either way, trade below the free-market quantity creates a deadweight loss — value that would have been created by trades that no longer happen.
