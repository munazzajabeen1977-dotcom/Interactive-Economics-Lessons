Move along the frontier — every extra unit of A means giving up some B
More resources or better technology shifts the whole frontier outward — growth doesn't erase trade-offs, it just relaxes them
How to read it: every point on the curve uses all resources efficiently — more of one good always means less of the other. Points inside the curve are achievable but wasteful (idle factories, unemployed workers). Points outside the curve are simply unreachable with current resources and technology. The opportunity cost of an extra unit of A is exactly how much B you must give up — and because the frontier bows outward, that cost rises the more of A you already have, since resources best suited to B get pulled in to make more A.
